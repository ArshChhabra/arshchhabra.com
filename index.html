About

I am an MS in Accounting and Fundamental Analysis (MSAFA) student at Columbia Business School, graduating in December 2027, focused on investment banking, private equity, and intrinsic valuation. I am the elected Academic Representative for my cohort and a member of CSIMA and the Restructuring & Debt Investing Club. I passed CFA Level I in August 2026 and am a Level II candidate.

Most recently I was a Private Equity Summer Analyst at 360 ONE Asset Management in Mumbai, working on growth-stage and late-stage investments. I worked on live deals across the transaction life cycle, helped write investment committee notes, and conducted commercial due diligence and research.

Before that, I began the doctoral program in Operations Research and Financial Engineering at Princeton on a full fellowship, and withdrew voluntarily to pursue a career in applied finance. The program deepened my interest in financial modeling, probability, and the mathematics of how capital moves.

I studied Mathematics and Computer Science at Pomona College, where I received the Hugh J. Hamilton Prize in Mathematics and the John Stauffer Scholarship for Academic Merit, was elected to Phi Beta Kappa and Sigma Xi, and published three peer-reviewed papers spanning linear algebra, number theory, abstract algebra, and combinatorics. I was also a teaching assistant for six courses across five semesters, from abstract algebra to statistical theory, and for the 121 Math Summer Program.

Outside of markets, I read widely, play drums, and think too much about technological entropy.

Writing on Markets

Independent research based only on public information.

  • Deferred Distress: Apparent Stability in the US Residential Real Estate Market

    US house prices look stable and delinquency sits below 2019 levels, even as affordability is near multi-decade lows. The essay argues that this stability partly reflects how distress is measured and recognized, tracing three mechanisms: affordability pressure that headline data hides, accounting rules that delay loss recognition, and incentives that break the chain from a missed payment to a price index.

  • Adaptive Corporate Governance and the Cost of Capital: Market Structure of India's Renewable IPP Sector

    A study of market structure in India's utility-scale renewable IPP sector. It argues that corporate governance is scale-conditional, and that the cost of capital is the mechanism connecting small-scale value destruction to large-scale value creation. It closes with a convergence trade and two deployment ideas for private capital.

Mathematics

Peer-reviewed papers from undergraduate research at Pomona College.

  • Numerical Semigroups from Rational Matrices III: Semigroups of Matricial Dimension Two and a Counterexample to the Lonely Element Conjecture

    We characterize semigroups of the natural numbers of matricial dimension 2 and produce a counterexample to the conjecture that a numerical semigroup whose small elements are lonely has matricial dimension at most 2.

    Arsh Chhabra and Stephan Ramon Garcia

    Communications in Algebra, 2025 · Journal · arXiv

  • Numerical Semigroups from Rational Matrices II: Matricial Dimension Does Not Exceed Multiplicity

    We continue our study of exponent semigroups of rational matrices. Our main result is that the matricial dimension of a numerical semigroup is at most its multiplicity (the least generator), greatly improving upon the previous upper bound (the conductor). For many numerical semigroups, including all symmetric numerical semigroups, our upper bound is tight.

    Arsh Chhabra, Stephan Ramon Garcia, and Christopher O'Neill

    Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society, 2024 · Journal · arXiv

  • Numerical Semigroups from Rational Matrices I: Power-Integral Matrices and Nilpotent Representations

    Our aim in this paper is to initiate the study of exponent semigroups for rational matrices. We prove that every numerical semigroup is the exponent semigroup of some rational matrix. We also obtain lower bounds on the size of such matrices and discuss the related class of power-integral matrices.

    Arsh Chhabra, Stephan Ramon Garcia, Fangqian Zhang, and Hechun Zhang

    Communications in Algebra, 2024 · Journal · arXiv

Research Presentations

Adinkras as Origami

Adinkras are bipartite graphs introduced by physicists to study supersymmetry, and are known to be a type of Dessin d'Enfant. The project studied how to express this construction through an elliptic curve, so that the resulting map is an "origami": a square tiling of a surface branched over a single point.

Awarded a fully funded research fellowship through the PRiME REU (Pomona Research in Math Experience, a Research Experience for Undergraduates), under the supervision of Prof. Edray Goins. Presented a poster at MAA MathFest 2023 that won honorable mention, and presented findings in an hour-long talk at Pomona College with the team.
